Listing Lens: the right facts for the exact flat.

We put unit-level facts beside the listing a person is already viewing. On 2,210 leases from April and May 2026, held out of training, our typical weekly miss was $52.44. The published Microburbs rent baseline is $86, and a suburb median is $100.

Code and install: github.com/cryptotweezer/Microburbs_Hackathon. Task 3 has no scored CSV. Task 4 is rents_mesh-block-party.csv, 65,777 flats. These figures come from the repo and the team's checks, not live listing visits.

Task 3 · Chrome extension

What buyers already see, and what we add

realestate.com.au and Domain already show the listing: price or contact, beds, baths, cars, photos, map, agent, and inspections, plus suburb medians on another page. We did not audit named Chrome extensions, so we do not claim they lack a feature. Overlays in this category usually repeat the listing or add a suburb summary. The failure that matters is a fact about the wrong home.

Why we chose these facts

The person is deciding whether to enquire. These are the questions the listing page does not answer for this unit.

Price and rent. Asking price against the Microburbs valuation, a same-building sale, and nearby sales. A rental uses our model, with a car space and without.

Confidence. High, Medium, or Low comes from the method. High is the valuation or our rent model. Medium is a same-building sale. Low is nearby comparables only. No estimate means no ring. A missing tile says "No data".

Local context. Planning applications, public housing, flood, heritage, and the nearest station or stop. An overlay is not an inspection. Public housing is a nearby-area figure, shown in the list and left out of the score.

Your match. A rental is scored as a renter. A sale asks to live in or to invest. The score stays hidden until a slider moves or the suggested settings are chosen. Weights are 0 to 5. Missing factors are skipped. 70 and above is a strong fit, 45 to 69 is fair, and below 45 is weak. Settings stay in the browser.

Assistant. Microburbs answers cite their source. Repayments are a separate Listing Lens calculator: principal and interest, defaulting to a 20% deposit, 6% interest, and 30 years. It can add median strata. It excludes stamp duty, fees, and later rate changes.

Data, method, and privacy

Address only → One G-NAF unit → API facts

The page gives up an address only: JSON-LD, then the title, then the URL. Listing text and photos are not collected. You can type an address. Suburb, number, street, and unit must all match, including B401 and 401B. Anything less loads no facts. The key stays on the server. Each overview figure keeps its source and date. Match settings stay in the browser.

What worked, and what did not

Exact match, a source on each number, and fair rent are in the product. Tests cover address fixtures, not live portal pages. There is no log of judged listing URLs.

There was too much back and forth on which features to build. We could have started iterating sooner.

The listings index misses some ads in the largest suburbs, so those fall through to the geocoder with no asking price. The valuation is often missing, so many sales show Low confidence.

What we found along the way

A flat's own earlier rent is used when it exists, then the building, suburb, and city. Parking is a separate scenario. On 1,777 February–March leases the median blend missed by $64.63, and the LightGBM step was worse at $68.84. It helped the later fold only.

What our separate check showed

April–May features stop at 31 March. February–March features stop at 31 January. Nothing after 31 July 2026 is used. Synthetic runs are not these scores.

If a holdout ad has no parking flag, the internal score uses the midpoint of the two rents. The file itself has both rents for every flat. The hidden file was not used.

What data supported the result?

Flat, lease, and block files. Required fields are G-NAF id, building, suburb, block, bedrooms, advert date, and weekly rent. Bathrooms, parking, floor, and size are optional.

The file has 65,777 unique ids, both rents on every row, no blanks, and no reversed pairs.

How did we estimate rent?

M0 medians → M1 last rent → M2 residual

M0 is a time-adjusted median, from the building down to a global median. M1 blends the unit's last rent with that. M2 fits LightGBM to the leftover error on three earlier windows, and falls back to M1 under 100 rows. Parking gaps are observed, not causal. Rents are rounded to the nearest 50 cents.

What remains uncertain?

The score that counts is the hidden August–September file. Asking rent is not a signed lease, and condition or outlook can still miss by more than $100. The February–March fold already showed that.

Next we would add a map without loosening the match, explain a missing fact instead of a blank tile, and test any model change on a new dated cohort.
